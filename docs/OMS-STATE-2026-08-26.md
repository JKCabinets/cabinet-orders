# 1. The shape of the thing

A Shopify storefront (`jkcabinets2you.com`, **not yet live**) and a custom
order-management system (`ordersjkcabinets2you.com`). Next.js 16, Supabase,
Kamal → Docker → one Hetzner box. Small internal team, Queen Creek Arizona,
`America/Phoenix` throughout.

**No staging environment.** Every deploy goes straight to production.

## ⚠ The OMS is no longer entirely behind auth

Added 2026-09-08. Two endpoints answer anonymous requests from the storefront:

| Route | For | Writes? |
|---|---|---|
| `POST /api/public/lookup` | "where is my order" | no |
| `POST /api/public/claims` | the warranty claim form | yes |

⚠ **Everything under `app/api/public/` is unauthenticated BY CONSTRUCTION.**
`proxy.ts` exempts the directory, not the files, so adding a route there makes
it public with no second decision anywhere. That is the point of the directory
and also the hazard.

⚠ **Both run as the SERVICE ROLE**, like every route on this box — full table
access, RLS bypassed. The `public_api` role with narrow column grants was
designed to be the real boundary and **does not exist**. Until it does, the
explicit select list in each route is the only thing keeping `customer_phone`,
`ship_to` and `internal_notes` out of a public response. Treat those select
lists as a security control.

⚠ **They fail in opposite directions, deliberately.** The lookup's rate limiter
fails CLOSED: a Redis outage letting reads through turns it into an order
number oracle. The claims intake fails OPEN: refusing a claim during an outage
costs a customer their claim, inside a window Terms 12.3 makes a condition
precedent. Spam is recoverable; a missed deadline is not.

# 2. ⚠ The data model — read this before anything else

## A checkout is a PROJECT. Its lines split into GROUPS.

This is the single most important thing to understand, and the thing most older
documents get wrong.

```
projects           one row per Shopify checkout
  └── orders       one row per PRODUCT CATEGORY in that checkout
```

One customer buying cabinets and a sample produces **one project and two
`orders` rows** — a cabinets group and a samples group. Each has its own stage,
its own SLA clock, its own timeline. A project whose cabinets are in production
while its samples are delivered is **normal**, and any summary that flattens
those into one status is hiding the thing the model exists to show.

Splitting is by **line-item vendor**, per line, in `lib/categories.ts`. An
unmatched vendor falls through to `order` deliberately, so the line lands in a
queue somebody works, and it is logged.

⚠ **Older text saying "a sample is an order whose every line is the sample
vendor, mixed orders are standard" describes the pre-project model and is
wrong.** It appears in more than one place.

⚠ **THIS MODEL DECIDES THE PUBLIC LOOKUP.** One project can have cabinets in
production and samples already delivered at the same time, so a lookup cannot
return one status. The customer-facing contract lives in OPERATIONS §2.

## Five types

| `type` | What it is | Has a project? |
|---|---|---|
| `order` | Cabinets | yes |
| `hardware` | Hardware | yes |
| `sample` | Samples | yes |
| `custom` | Quote-form or manual job | **no — standalone** |
| `warranty` | Claim about a purchase | **no — standalone** |

That project/standalone split decides three things: **archiving**, **claiming**
and **money**. See §3.

⚠ **A `warranty` row is standalone but never orphaned.** `about_order_id` is
required and points at the **GROUP** it is about — `SHO-1050-CAB`, not
`SHO-1050` — because the 48-hour reporting window in Terms 12.3 runs from a
delivery, and deliveries are per group. `reported_at` carries the customer's
submission time, not the row's creation time.

## Order numbers

Customer sees `ORDER #1050`. The OMS project is `SHO-1050`. Its groups are
`SHO-1050-CAB`, `SHO-1050-HW`, `SHO-1050-SMP`. Shopify's internal id is never
customer-facing. Custom jobs keep a `QUO-` prefix, which encodes origin rather
than type.

**Warranty claims are `WRN-1050-1`, `WRN-1050-2`.** Added 2026-09-08. Generated
in one place, `lib/warrantyId.ts`.

⚠ **The sequence is per PURCHASE, not per group.** `SHO-1050-CAB` and
`SHO-1050-SMP` both key on `1050` and share one sequence, so a claim on the
cabinets and a claim on the samples are `-1` and `-2`. `lib/createWarranty.ts`
counts by id pattern for that reason; counting by `about_order_id` would give
both `-1` and leave the conflict retry to notice.

⚠ **The suffix is ALWAYS present, even on the first claim.** If `-1` were
implicit, every consumer would have to handle two shapes, and the second claim
on an order is exactly when somebody discovers it.

⚠ **Every claim is its own row.** Two claims on one order are independent: each
has its own dates and lead times regardless of when it was raised. Same
reasoning as cabinets and samples being separate groups under one project,
applied one level down.

## ⚠ A customer submission is a DRAFT, not a claim

Added 2026-09-08. `claim_submissions` holds what a customer sent through the
website. It is **not** a row in `orders`, has no id, no stage of its own and no
SLA clock.

It appears on `/warranty` above the claims table, at `New claim` — which is what
that stage means: nobody has worked it yet. A team member opens it, confirms
which GROUP it is about, and records what actually needs replacing. Creating the
claim is that act, and it produces the `warranty` row.

**Both records are kept.** The customer says "my base drawer cabinet has a dent
in the drawer front"; a vendor cannot fill that. Staff write "W1842-580F-PN,
Middle Drawer Only, qty 1". The submission is what they actually reported,
`received_at` carries legal weight under Terms 12.3, and editing a report in
place is how the report gets lost.

⚠ **Drafts are never shaped into `Order` objects.** The orders hub feeds its
rows to the table, the bulk action bar, the modal and the stage-move path. A
draft wearing an `Order`'s shape would reach all four, and a bulk stage advance
would be advancing something that has no row.

**A manual claim is the same screen with nothing prefilled** — for a mistake on
JK's side needing a replacement, where there is no customer report to work
from. One flow, one create path, one id sequence.

# 3. The five pipelines

⚠ **Stage names are NOT globally unique.** `New` and `Delivered` appear in
several flows; `In review` is in both warranty and custom; **`Shipped` is in
three**. Always resolve against the row's `type` — never by searching arrays
blind. This has caused real incidents.

| Flow | Stages |
|---|---|
| **Cabinets** | New → Entered → In production → At cross dock → Delivered |
| **Hardware** | New → Ordered → Shipped → Delivered |
| **Samples** | New → Shipped → Delivered |
| **Custom** | New → In review → Ordered → In production → At cross dock → Delivered |
| **Warranty** | New claim → In review → Parts ordered → Shipped → Resolved |

**How each moves:**

- **Cabinets.** New → Entered is human, gated on a manufacturer acknowledgment
  or an attachment. Entered → In production is automatic when a production start
  date is set. In production → At cross dock is the `production-complete` cron,
  its sole owner. At cross dock → Delivered is human and **gated on a signed
  proof-of-delivery attachment**, overridable with a logged reason.

  ⚠ **Both overrides take a reason and write an activity row** (2026-09-15).
  The control is accountability, not permission: anyone may override, and
  everyone can see who did. `override_ack` was a bare boolean that wrote
  nothing — no reason, no name, no trail — for the whole of its life until
  then, and OPERATIONS §10 had recorded the inconsistency as known-wrong.

  ⚠ **The flag also used to sit INSIDE the gate's condition**
  (`&& !body.override_ack`), so an override skipped the check entirely: a
  row whose acknowledgment was green recorded a bypass of a check that
  would have passed. The check runs first now, and the reason is consulted
  only when it actually fails.

  ⚠ **"An acknowledgment" means a GREEN one that still describes the order.**
  Added 2026-08-27. Every acknowledgment stores a fingerprint of exactly what
  the reconciliation compared — normalised customer name, normalised ship-to,
  and per-SKU quantities and modifications. The gate recomputes it and a
  mismatch blocks the advance: a confirmation of lines that have since changed
  is not a confirmation of this order.

  ⚠ **THE UI NAMES THE AUTOMATIC MOVE** (2026-09-15). A row In production with
  an estimated finish date says so — "Moves to At cross dock on 09/18/2026,
  when production ends" — and its button reads **Manual Push**, because
  pressing it takes the transition early rather than being the only way
  forward. `lib/autoAdvance.ts` holds the cron's rule and BOTH import it: a
  promise made where the cron will not keep it is worse than silence, because
  somebody waits for it. Without a finish date the cron's query never matches,
  so the row sits indefinitely and the wording stays neutral.

  ⚠ **Entered → In production is GATED on a production start date.** Added
  2026-09-08. Setting the date auto-advances the row, and that used to be the
  whole rule — nothing refused a manual advance without it, so a row could
  reach In production where `production-complete` could never move it, because
  that cron advances on `production_est_finish_date <= today`.

  The manufacturer gives dates within a day or two of the order being placed,
  well before production starts, so a row here without them is a skipped step
  rather than an honest unknown. The gate accepts the date in the same request
  as the stage, because that is what the modal sends.

  ⚠ **A stale acknowledgment does NOT move an order backwards.** If a customer
  amends an order that has already advanced, the row stays where it is and asks
  for a fresh acknowledgment. Reverting a stage as a side effect of a phone call
  is not a decision the system should take.

  ⚠ **The gate is LOOSER than the wording suggests, and that is DECIDED.**
  The server passes on a green acknowledgment **or any attachment at all** —
  the file need not be an acknowledgment. Kept loose deliberately
  (2026-09-08): HCI and J&K change their order forms constantly, so a parser
  for them would be a gate that breaks on somebody else's schedule. The
  in-app wording names both remedies. `OPERATIONS` §12 carries the reasoning.
- **Hardware and samples.** ⚠ **A TRACKING NUMBER IS WHAT MAKES A GROUP
  "Shipped"** — not a button. Entering one in the modal advances it; so does a
  Shopify fulfilment carrying one. One rule, both doors. Delivered stays a human
  action: nothing tells us a parcel arrived. Clearing a number does not move the
  row back.

  ⚠ **But a backward move CLEARS the number.** Added 2026-08-27. The number is
  the evidence for "Shipped", so a row sitting before that stage cannot hold it
  — and while it did, the SLA clock stayed off for exactly the row that needed
  chasing, because that clock runs on the number being absent.
- **Custom.** Hand-driven end to end. Dates are typed in after a phone call and
  nothing automated reads them. **No receipt gate on At cross dock →
  Delivered** (2026-09-09): a custom job runs on the customer's own contract
  and purchase order, so Terms 12.3 does not reach a custom job and the signed
  proof of delivery that evidences it is not required. Both copies of the
  gate ask `lib/requirements`, whose custom block is the record of this.

  ⚠ **More generally: a custom job has no gates at all** (2026-09-09). It is
  an organisation tool, and its dates are a record of a phone call rather
  than a condition of anything. **That applies to the UI too** — no control
  may withhold itself pending a custom date and no copy may say one unlocks
  a step. Three did until 2026-09-09, all at At cross dock, and a demand
  nothing enforces is worse than a gate: there is no refusal to search for,
  only a button that never appears.

**Shipping reality:** cabinets go by freight to a cross dock (**no tracking
number, ever**). Hardware is drop-ship — placed with the manufacturer, who ships
direct and supplies the tracking number. Samples ship from JK's own stock,
usually same day, and **their label is bought inside Shopify**, so the Shopify
fulfilment is the primary door for a sample's tracking number.

⚠ **Whether a HARDWARE tracking number ever reaches Shopify is undecided.** It
depends on the vendor and on whether they are integrated with it. "Ships via
UPS" and "the fulfilment carries real carrier and tracking" were written into
`lib/categories.ts` as fact on 2026-08-25 and were assumptions; the comments now
say so. No hardware product is live, so nothing has tested any of it.

## ⚠ One truth per item

Stated once, because it now decides several behaviours. **The row holds the
current truth; the activity log holds the history.** A backward move clears
whatever the stage it returns to will demand again, so re-entry is always a
fresh recording — even when the value is identical, because what is recorded is
a new decision rather than the same one twice. Anything worth keeping for
documentation goes in attachments or internal notes; the OMS does not accumulate
every version of a fact.

## ⚠ Dates: stored ISO, displayed MM/DD/YYYY

Added 2026-09-15. The DATE columns — `production_start_date`,
`production_est_finish_date`, `scheduled_delivery_date` — are stored ISO and
rendered through `formatMDY` in `lib/dates.ts`.

⚠ **IT PARSES THE STRING RATHER THAN USING `new Date()`.** These are DATE,
not timestamp: `new Date("2026-09-16")` is UTC midnight, which in Phoenix is
5pm on the 15th, so every date in the app would render a day early and look
like a data fault.

⚠ **`orders.date` AND `order_activity.time` ARE NOT ISO.** They are stored as
DISPLAY strings ("Sep 14"), written that way at ingest, so they still read
that way and `formatMDY` returns them untouched. Making them consistent means
changing what is written, not the renderer — and `order_activity.time` having
no clock component is also why the pipeline rail cannot show per-stage
timestamps.

The corollary is the activity log's guarantee: **every PATCH records everything
it changed.** Not one row per field — a stage move caused by a date is one row
describing both — but nothing changes silently, and nothing is claimed that did
not happen.

## Ownership, archiving, money

**One owner per PURCHASE.** A project is claimed as a whole, so a designer who
has finished the cabinets is not blocked from closing it while somebody else
sits on the hardware. Custom jobs and warranty claims are claimed individually,
having no project. Claiming happens on `/projects` or the work queue, never from
a stage table — a Claim button on a cabinet row would quietly take the samples
too.

⚠ **A CLAIM IS ENFORCED, NOT DISPLAYED** (2026-09-15). Until then it was
shown in three places and checked in none: any signed-in member could move,
date, annotate or upload against a row somebody else had claimed. A claim
that only shows an avatar does not do the one thing claims exist for.

`requireOrderClaim` in `lib/auth.ts` is the single answer, called by the
PATCH route, attachment upload, attachment delete and acknowledgment upload.
Unclaimed is open — claiming is how you take a row, and requiring one before
any edit would make every first touch a two-step. Another member's claim is
refused with 409 `claimed_by_other`.

⚠ **ADMINS ARE EXEMPT UNCONDITIONALLY, AND THE GUARD LOGS IT.** The modal's
"Edit order" toggle is deliberateness in the UI, not enforcement — an
admin's request succeeds whether or not it has been pressed. Do not "fix"
the server to require it: an admin acting through the API, a script or a
stale tab must still be able to act, and must still land on the activity
trail. The guard writes that row itself rather than trusting four callers
to remember.

⚠ **OWNERSHIP RESOLVES THROUGH THE PROJECT.** `orders.claimed_by` is null on
every project-linked row since the claim moved up on 2026-08-25, so a check
reading it raw finds no owner on any Shopify purchase — it would enforce
nothing on the type with the most hands on it.

**Archived as a whole purchase**, and only once every group reaches the last
stage of its own flow, or the purchase is refunded. A refund is an ending.
The terminal-stage rule is enforced in `app/api/projects/[id]`, which refuses
with `not_complete` and names the unfinished groups.

⚠ **THE PROJECT IS THE ONLY TRUTH FOR ARCHIVING — decided and enforced
2026-09-15.** `orders.archived` stays false on project-linked rows; a group
is hidden because its PROJECT is archived, resolved by lookup. Writing both
is two copies of one fact. `PATCH /api/orders/[id]` refuses `archived` in
either direction on a project-linked row with 422 `archived_not_allowed`,
naming the project, and `/api/orders/bulk` refuses each such row the same
way. `/api/orders/archive`, a third order-level path that nothing called,
was deleted rather than given a refusal.

⚠ **THE REFUSAL SITS ABOVE THE CLAIM GUARD** in the PATCH route, because
`requireOrderClaim` writes the admin-override row: a refusal after it would
record an admin edit that never happened. Every other refusal in that route
still sits below the guard — an open item in the handoff.

**The UI follows through one question: `archivesAsOrder` in `lib/data`.**
The modal's Archive/Restore button, the table's Delivered action and the
bulk bar all ask it, so none of them offers one group of a purchase on its
own. The modal's button also needs the viewer to be able to act on the row,
matching the table. The store's `archiveOrder` reverts a refused change and
returns the server's reason; it used to discard it, which left the row gone
from the board until a refresh.

⚠ **THE DATABASE DOES NOT ENFORCE IT.** Only the routes do: a webhook, an
import, a script or the SQL editor can still write the flag on a group. A
CHECK constraint in the form of `orders_total_price_standalone_only` is
proposed and undecided.

That is how SHO-1052 vanished on 2026-09-15 — archived as a project AND as
two groups, then restored as a project only. It showed in the projects hub
and not in cabinet orders, because the two read different tables. **A
project is the unit of archiving: archive it and both groups go, restore it
and both come back.** Standalone rows — custom jobs and warranty claims,
which have no project — keep order-level archiving.

**Money lives on the project.** `projects.total_price` for checkouts;
`orders.total_price` only for standalone custom jobs, enforced by a CHECK so a
project-linked row physically cannot carry one. Revenue is the sum of both, with
no overlap possible.

## SLA — 24h soft, 48h hard

Two ideas, and the second is the one people miss:

- **Elapsed-time stages** ask "how long has this been sitting here". `New`
  measures from the order date, so bouncing an order back cannot reset it.
- **Missing-data stages** ask "is the thing that would let this move absent".
  An order can legitimately sit in production for six weeks — what is never
  legitimate is sitting there with no dates, because then nothing can advance
  it. Same for a hardware group with no tracking number.

Stages waiting on a third party — `Parts ordered`, `Shipped` — have **no rule**.
There is no field that would say when to stop worrying.

# 4. Why a row needs someone

One derivation, `lib/attention.ts`, read by the dashboard, the work queue and
the sidebar badges. **Nothing carries its own predicates**, because this
codebase's recurring failure is one rule written twice with a clause missing
from the second — it happened four times in six days before this existed.

Reasons: past SLA · due soon · blocked on missing data · unclaimed · payment
hold · acknowledgment missing · receipt missing.

## ⚠ `lib/requirements.ts` is the source for what a row still needs

Added 2026-09-08. One table keyed on `(type, stage)`. Each entry carries a
label, a remedy, a predicate, whether being unmet keeps an SLA clock running
(`clocks`), and — since 2026-09-09 — whether the SERVER refuses the
transition without it (`gates`).

⚠ **`clocks` AND `gates` ARE DIFFERENT QUESTIONS.** A delivery date at At
cross dock keeps a clock running and the route lets the move through without
it; a signed receipt runs no clock and the route refuses without it. The
modal disables its move button on `gates` and only `gates` — disabling on
every unmet requirement would be a client gate stricter than its server,
which is the failure this table exists to end.

⚠ **THREE FUNCTIONS, THREE QUESTIONS.** Reaching for the wrong one is subtle
and has already produced one near-miss:

| Function | Asks |
|---|---|
| `requirementsFor(order)` | what is the CURRENT stage waiting on |
| `dateControlsFor(order)` | is a date the thing you came here to TYPE |
| `typeEverRequires(type, id)` | does this type need it at ANY stage |

`dateControlsFor` exists because "required" and "is the next action" are not
the same: a custom job requires nothing by decision and still wants its
production dates recorded, and conflating the two left it with no field at
all. `typeEverRequires` exists because the tracking gate applies **whichever
direction you arrive from**, and the table lists `tracking_number` at the
stage BEFORE Shipped — so a current-stage question would silently stop
requiring it on a backward admin move from Delivered, which is exactly when
somebody is fixing a mistake.

⚠ **`lib/requirements.ts` KEEPS NO RUNTIME DEPENDENCY ON `lib/data`.** The
dev-only consistency check uses a lazy import for that reason, and
`typeEverRequires` resolves flows from the table's own keys rather than the
stage list. `REQUIREMENTS` is exhaustive over every `(type, stage)` — custom
lists all six of its stages with empty arrays — and the boot check fails if
that stops being true.

Before it, the same question was answered in FOUR places — `sla.ts`'s
`clockRuns`/`waitingFor`, `stageLogic.ts`'s `fieldsToClearOnBackwardMove`, the
PATCH gates, and `categories.ts`'s `trackingTargetStage`. Four shapes of one
fact.

**Deriving from it today:** `sla.ts` (`clockRuns` and `waitingFor` are overlaid
in `slaRuleFor`, so no caller changed), `attention.ts` (the two join-backed
reasons), the dashboard and the work queue.

**The PATCH gates: DONE 2026-09-15.** All four — acknowledgment-or-attachment,
production start date, tracking number, signed receipt — derive from the
table on the server, and `lib/stageGates.ts`'s client copy asks the same
line. Verified by enumerating every (type, from-stage) pair: **69 of 69
identical.** Do that for anything else migrated here; reading the diff is
not the same evidence.

⚠ **`gates` IS DESCRIPTIVE, NOT EXECUTABLE.** The production gate still reads
the request body: `requirementsFor` answers from the STORED row, but the
modal sends the date and the stage in one PATCH, so a fully table-driven
check would refuse the very request that satisfies it. The table can say a
requirement blocks a transition; it cannot say "and the request itself may
supply it". **Do not try to finish the job by removing that.**

**Not yet:** `fieldsToClearOnBackwardMove`. A behaviour change, to be argued
on its own.

⚠ **THREE STATES, NOT TWO.** `met`, `unmet`, `unknown`. Unknown is what a
join-backed requirement reports when nobody fetched the enrichment — and the
right response to it differs by surface. A QUEUE shows no reason, because one
that guesses sends somebody to redo finished work. A MODAL shows it as
outstanding with the move button disabled, because a person is looking straight
at it and a premature tick beside a live button is the worse error.

## Answering the joins: `POST /api/orders/enrichment`

Returns `{ackGreen, hasAttachment, hasProofOfDelivery}` per order, in **five
queries regardless of how many rows are asked for**. `lib/useOrderEnrichment.ts`
is the hook; the work queue, the dashboard and the modal all call it, so they
cannot disagree.

⚠ **It reimplements nothing.** The four-layer vendor chain is `resolveVendors`
and the green-and-not-stale verdict is `allVendorsGreen` — the same functions
the server gate calls. A second copy of either would mean the queue and the
gate disagreeing about whether an order can advance, and the queue is the one
people would believe.

⚠ **The last two have never appeared on a screen.** *(Fixed 2026-09-08 — see
above. Kept because the reasoning below is why the enrichment argument exists
at all.)* They need a join —
`order_acknowledgments` and `order_attachments` cannot be read off the row — so
`attentionFor` takes them through an optional `enrich` argument. **No caller
passes it.** Every call site in the dashboard and the work queue calls
`attentionFor(order)` with one argument, so those two reasons are unreachable:
built, documented, and never fired. Making them live needs a batch endpoint
rather than a per-row query, and it is item 1 on the remaining-work list.

⚠ **The buckets OVERLAP on purpose.** A purchase blocked for sixty hours counts
as blocked AND past-SLA AND needing attention. They are four questions about the
same rows, not a partition — making them exclusive would drop a row out of
"Blocked" the moment it also breached, which is when you most want to see it.

# 5. ⚠ Realtime — which tables publish, and why it matters

Four tables are in the `supabase_realtime` publication: **`orders`,
`projects`, `team_members`, `order_activity`.** Subscriptions live in
`lib/useRealtimeOrders.ts`, one channel each, wired in `lib/store.tsx`.

⚠ **`projects` WAS MISSING UNTIL 2026-09-15**, and the subscription had been
correct the whole time with nothing to receive. Because a Shopify
purchase's claim lives on the PROJECT, a claim taken by one person reached
nobody else's screen until they happened to refresh — which defeats the
entire purpose of claiming. Recorded as
`migrations/2026-09-14-realtime-projects.sql`.

⚠ **ADDING A TABLE TO THE PUBLICATION WITH NO SUBSCRIBER DOES NOTHING** and
looks like a fix. `order_attachments` is still absent for exactly that
reason: the store holds no attachment state and the panel fetches its own
list, so it needs the client change first. Follow `lib/ackStatus.ts` — a
module-level cache with per-order subscriber sets and an `invalidate`.

⚠ **THE ACTIVITY MERGE IS DEDUPED.** `moveStage` and friends append an entry
optimistically before the round-trip, so realtime delivering the server's
row would show the actor their own action twice. Matched on text AND time —
and `time` is a DAY string, so two identical texts on one day collapse to
one. Entry texts carry a stage and an actor name, which makes that unlikely;
dropping the optimistic append instead would put a round-trip in front of
every action's feedback.

**healthchecks.io**, deliberately off-box so a box outage cannot silence its own
alarm. Four cron jobs, each pinging a check after it runs.

| UTC | Job |
|---|---|
| 07:30 daily | `sync-avis-catalog` |
| 08:00 daily | `production-complete` |
| 14:00 weekdays | `teams-digest` (silent until `TEAMS_WEBHOOK_URL` is set) |

## ⚠ `webhook_events` — webhook outcomes that survive a deploy

Added 2026-09-15. `logWebhook` wrote to `console.warn` and nowhere else, in
a container replaced on every deploy. That is how the SHO-1051 deletion bug
survived two attempts: the handler reported `removed` twice while deleting
nothing, and the only evidence was log lines one more deploy would have
erased.

Every call site now also writes a row. The insert is **not awaited** — a
handler must not fail or slow because logging failed, and Shopify retries on
a non-2xx, so a logging error would become a redelivery loop for a webhook
that already did its work. It falls back to stderr if the write fails,
because a log table that quietly stops recording is the same shape as the
bug it replaces.

```sql
select outcome, topic, order_id, shopify_id, detail, created_at
from webhook_events order by created_at desc limit 20;
```

⚠ **The account holds eleven checks. Four watch anything.** The other seven are
strays from an earlier setup, each with a 365-day period, so they report "up"
forever. They are listed for deletion and ignored **by name** in the in-app
panel — not merely left ungrouped, because ungrouped is where a genuinely new
check would land.

## ⚠ What the ping path guarantees

- **`run-cron.sh` validates the URL shape before pinging.** A malformed entry
  goes to stderr, which cron mails.
- **A non-2xx ping is logged.**
- ⚠ **The job's exit status is untouched by either.** Monitoring must never
  break the thing it monitors.
- **The in-app panel is a window onto the alarm, never the alarm.** If the box
  is down, so is the OMS and so is the panel.

The first two are what the six-day monitoring outage of August 2026 produced.
The third was already the design and stays it — the bug was never the exit
status, it was that the ping path could not report on itself. **The incident
itself is in `OPERATIONS` §9**, which is where history lives.
